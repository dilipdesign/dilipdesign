01 / Context

Building a scalable platform for sports organizations.

This project involved designing an enterprise web application that enables sports organizations to efficiently manage tournaments, venues, teams, registrations, officials, finances, and administrative operations from a centralized platform.

The primary goal was to simplify complex operational workflows while creating a scalable design system that supports multiple user roles and growing business needs.

02 / Problem

Disconnected tools, growing operational complexity.

Sports organizations often relied on disconnected tools and manual processes to coordinate tournaments, registrations, scheduling, and operations. As the platform expanded, the challenge was to unify these workflows into an intuitive enterprise experience that could scale.

01

Scale & coordination

Administrators managed hundreds of concurrent events while coordinating venues and schedules across the organization.

02

Data & registrations

Large volumes of registrations needed to be processed while managing data consistently across multiple user roles.

03

Complex & inconsistent workflows

Navigating complex administrative workflows was made harder by inconsistent interfaces across the platform.

03 / Approach

Discovery, design, scale.

I led the UX strategy for the platform, moving from stakeholder discovery through to a scalable design system that could support continued product growth.

01

Led stakeholder interviews, mapped key workflow dependencies, and reviewed existing platform analytics to identify the highest-impact user journeys.

02

03

Scale

Built reusable UI components and patterns that supported future product expansion while ensuring accessibility and performance.

04 / Design Challenges

The biggest part of this engagement.

Event Scheduling

Managing multiple tournaments across different venues created scheduling conflicts and operational complexity.

Design Decision
  • Designed a calendar-driven scheduling experience that prevented venue conflicts
  • Improved schedule visibility
  • Supported multiple event types
  • Simplified editing and rescheduling

Impact — Reduced scheduling complexity and improved planning efficiency.

Event Management

Event creation involved numerous configuration options that overwhelmed users.

Design Decision
  • Introduced a step-by-step workflow
  • Logical grouping
  • Progressive disclosure
  • Contextual help
  • Reusable templates

Result — Made event setup faster while reducing cognitive load.

Complex Enterprise Tables

Administrators spent most of their time managing large datasets.

Design Decision
  • Configurable columns
  • Saved views
  • Bulk actions
  • Sticky headers
  • Pagination, sorting, search, export
  • Row actions

Why it matters — Power users could personalize their workspace without sacrificing consistency.

Advanced Filtering

Large datasets made finding information difficult.

Design Decision
  • Created an advanced filtering system supporting date ranges, event type, organization, venue, team, status
  • Saved filters and filter chips

This significantly improved discoverability for administrators.

Venue Management

Venue information included availability, facilities, capacity, scheduling, and location.

Design Decision

Designed a simplified venue management workflow that reduced unnecessary inputs while exposing advanced options only when needed.

Registration & Approval Flow

Registration involved multiple review and approval steps.

Design Decision
  • Designed a transparent approval workflow using status indicators, timeline, validation messages, review actions, and clear system feedback

This reduced uncertainty for administrators.

Enterprise Navigation

As more modules were introduced, navigation became increasingly complex.

Design Decision

Created a scalable navigation system that grouped related modules, improved discoverability, and reduced context switching for administrators.

Design System

To support rapid product growth, I developed reusable UI components and standardized interaction patterns.

The system included:

Design Focus Areas

Clarity, consistency and reduced cognitive load.

For a SaaS product of this scale, the focus was on clarity, consistency, and reducing cognitive load — prioritizing task-based navigation, progressive disclosure, and a single source of truth for event state across the platform.

01

Task-based navigation

Streamlined multi-step event setup and reinforced scalable component patterns for future releases.

02

Progressive disclosure

Actionable dashboard insights for operations teams, with advanced options surfaced only when needed.

03

Single source of truth

Unified administration and finance workflows around a consistent view of event state.

06 / Reflection

Looking back at the work.

Systems thinking for a growing enterprise product.

Over 1 year and 7 months as Senior UX Designer, this engagement moved from stakeholder discovery through to a full design system, spanning research, workshops, UX design, and ongoing design system maintenance.

The Design Challenges section was intentionally the largest part of the case study — scheduling, event management, enterprise tables, filtering, venue management, registration and approval flows, enterprise navigation, and the design system itself each required a distinct, considered decision.

Because the engagement remained confidential throughout, this case study focuses on the qualitative outcomes and practitioner benefits of the work rather than client-specific metrics or interface screens.

Ready to translate complex enterprise requirements into systems that feel simple — that is the core takeaway I carry forward from this project.
